# Universal Principles of Design — Lidwell, Holden, Butler

## Key Design Principles for Brand Identity

### 80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)
- 80% of effects come from 20% of causes
- Applied to branding: focus design energy on the 20% of touchpoints that create 80% of brand impressions
- Identify the critical few touchpoints, perfect those first

### Aesthetic-Usability Effect
- People perceive aesthetically pleasing designs as easier to use
- A beautiful brand identity creates a halo effect: customers assume the product/service is also high quality
- First impressions are disproportionately influenced by visual aesthetics

### Affordance
- The physical characteristics of an object that suggest how it should be used
- Applied to brand design: visual cues should signal what the brand offers
- A premium brand should LOOK premium (paper quality, spacing, typography weight)

### Archetypes (Visual)
- Universal patterns recognized across cultures
- The Hero archetype visually: bold lines, dynamic angles, powerful colors
- Brands like Harley-Davidson use the Rebel archetype visually: dark, rugged, unapologetic
- Visual archetypes create instant, subconscious recognition

### Color Theory
- **Warm colors** (red, orange, yellow): advance, stimulate, create urgency
- **Cool colors** (blue, green, purple): recede, calm, create trust
- **Saturation**: High = energetic, youthful; Low = sophisticated, mature
- **Value/Brightness**: Light = optimistic, airy; Dark = powerful, serious

### Consistency
- A design system must be applied consistently across all touchpoints
- Inconsistency erodes trust — if the brand looks different everywhere, it feels unreliable
- Style guides exist to enforce consistency, not to limit creativity

### Figure-Ground Relationship
- The brain separates visual elements into foreground (figure) and background (ground)
- Strong logos exploit figure-ground: the FedEx arrow, the Spartan Golf Club
- Negative space is a design tool, not wasted space

### Gestalt Principles
- **Proximity**: Elements close together are perceived as related
- **Similarity**: Elements that look alike are perceived as grouped
- **Continuity**: The eye follows smooth lines and curves
- **Closure**: The brain completes incomplete shapes
- Applied to brand: layout, icon design, and logo construction use Gestalt principles

### Hierarchy
- Visual hierarchy guides the eye through information in order of importance
- Size, color, contrast, position, and whitespace all create hierarchy
- Every brand touchpoint needs clear hierarchy: what should the viewer see first?

### Signal-to-Noise Ratio
- Maximize the ratio of relevant information to irrelevant information
- Applied to branding: strip away decorative clutter, keep only what communicates the brand
- The most powerful brands have the highest signal-to-noise ratio (Apple, Nike)

## Norman's Design Principles (The Design of Everyday Things)

### Human-Centered Design (HCD)
- Design for real humans, not idealized users
- Observe how people actually interact with the brand (not how you hope they will)
- Iterate based on real feedback, not assumptions

### Signifiers
- Make important information visible
- In brand identity: the logo, color palette, and typography are signifiers of brand membership
- Every touchpoint should clearly signal "this belongs to [brand]"

### Mapping
- The relationship between controls and their effects should be natural
- In brand identity: the visual language should naturally map to the brand's personality
- If the brand is premium, every visual element should feel premium (no disconnect)

### Feedback
- Confirm that an action has been received and processed
- In brand identity: interactive touchpoints (website, app) must give clear visual feedback
- Brand consistency IS feedback — it confirms "you're still with us"

## Application to Brand Identity Design
- Use the 80/20 rule to prioritize which touchpoints to design first
- Exploit the aesthetic-usability effect: beautiful identity = perceived quality
- Apply Gestalt principles to logo and layout design
- Maximize signal-to-noise: every visual element must earn its place
- Use HCD: test the identity with real users, not just designers

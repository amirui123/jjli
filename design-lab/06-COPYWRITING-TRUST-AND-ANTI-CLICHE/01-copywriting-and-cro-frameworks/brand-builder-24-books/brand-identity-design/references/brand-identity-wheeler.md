# Brand Identity Process — Alina Wheeler (Designing Brand Identity)

## Wheeler's 4-Phase Process

### Phase 1: Conducting Research
- Clarify vision, strategies, goals, and values
- Research stakeholders, markets, competition, technology, legal
- Audit existing brand (if applicable): what works, what doesn't
- Interview stakeholders, customers, and employees
- **Deliverable**: Brand brief summarizing strategic foundation

### Phase 2: Clarifying Strategy
- Synthesize research into clear strategic positioning
- Define the brand's unique value proposition
- Develop the brand architecture (if multi-brand)
- Establish naming strategy and criteria
- **Deliverable**: Positioning statement and brand architecture

### Phase 3: Designing Identity
- Design the visual identity system:
  - Logo and logo system (primary, secondary, icon)
  - Color palette (primary, secondary, accent)
  - Typography (primary, secondary, hierarchy)
  - Imagery style (photography, illustration, iconography)
  - Design elements (patterns, textures, graphic devices)
- Develop identity across touchpoints (see below)
- **Deliverable**: Brand identity system presentation

### Phase 4: Creating Touchpoints
- Apply the identity system to all brand touchpoints
- Develop brand guidelines and standards
- Create templates and tools for consistent application
- Launch the brand internally first, then externally
- **Deliverable**: Brand guidelines + touchpoint templates

## Brand Ideals Framework

Wheeler identifies the essential qualities of an effective brand identity:

1. **Bold**: Stands for something — not timid or generic
2. **Memorable**: Leaves a lasting impression through distinctive visual cues
3. **Appropriate**: Feels right for the industry, audience, and brand personality
4. **Sustainable**: Built to last — not dependent on trends
5. **Flexible**: Works across media, cultures, and applications
6. **Coherent**: All elements feel like they belong to the same family
7. **Easy to apply**: Templates and guidelines enable consistent execution
8. **Meaningful**: Conveys the brand's purpose and values visually

## Touchpoint Inventory

Wheeler categorizes brand touchpoints as:

### Digital Touchpoints
- Website, mobile app, social media profiles
- Email signatures, newsletters, digital ads
- App store presence, SEO metadata

### Marketing Touchpoints
- Advertising (print, digital, outdoor)
- Brochures, sell sheets, presentations
- Trade show displays, event materials

### Stationery & Business
- Business cards, letterhead, envelopes
- Invoices, proposals, contracts
- ID badges, packaging

### Environmental
- Signage (exterior, interior, wayfinding)
- Retail/office interior design
- Vehicle graphics, uniforms

### Product & Packaging
- Product design integration
- Packaging (primary, secondary, shipping)
- Unboxing experience

## Brand Architecture Models
- **Monolithic** (Branded House): One master brand — Google, Virgin, Apple
- **Endorsed**: Sub-brands endorsed by parent — Marriott Courtyard, Polo by Ralph Lauren
- **Pluralistic** (House of Brands): Independent brands — P&G, Unilever, LVMH

## Application to Brand Identity Design
- Follow the 4-phase process: Research → Strategy → Design → Touchpoints
- Map ALL touchpoints before designing — ensures the system works everywhere
- Brand identity is a SYSTEM, not a single logo — every element must be coherent
- Launch internally first — employees must live the brand before customers experience it

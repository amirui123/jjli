# Logo Design Principles — David Airey (Logo Design Love)

## The Logo Design Process

### Phase 1: Research
- Understand the client's business, industry, competition, and audience
- Study competitors' logos to identify visual conventions AND white space
- Define the brand personality before touching design tools
- Ask: "What should people FEEL when they see this logo?"

### Phase 2: Sketch
- Generate 50-100+ rough concepts on paper
- Don't jump to the computer — digital tools constrain thinking too early
- Explore multiple directions: literal, abstract, typographic, symbolic
- Thumbnail sketches are about IDEAS, not polish

### Phase 3: Refine
- Select the 3-5 strongest concepts for digital refinement
- Work in black and white first — color is a distraction at this stage
- Test at multiple sizes: favicon (16px), app icon, business card, billboard
- Simplify ruthlessly — remove every element that doesn't earn its place

### Phase 4: Present
- Present 1-3 options (not 10 — that's indecisive, not generous)
- Show each logo in context: on a business card, website, product, app icon
- Explain the thinking behind each design — design is not decoration
- Let the work breathe — don't oversell

## Logo Design Principles

### Simplicity
- The best logos are simple enough to describe in one sentence
- Complexity = harder to remember, harder to reproduce, harder to scale
- Iconic logos (Nike swoosh, Apple, Target) are brutally simple

### Relevance
- The logo should feel appropriate for the brand's industry and personality
- It doesn't need to literally depict what the company does
- Apple's logo isn't a computer. Nike's swoosh isn't a shoe. But they FEEL right.

### Distinctiveness
- The "squint test": still recognizable when blurry or small
- Must stand out from competitors in the same industry
- Avoid trends — trendy logos look dated in 5 years

### Timelessness
- Ask: "Will this still work in 20 years?"
- Classic shapes and proportions age better than effects (gradients, 3D, shadows)
- Evolution over revolution — update subtly, don't redesign from scratch

### Versatility
- Works on dark and light backgrounds
- Works in one color (monochrome)
- Works at extreme sizes (tiny favicon, large signage)
- Works in different media (screen, print, embroidery, engraving)

### Memorability
- Can someone draw it from memory after seeing it once?
- The best logos are "sticky" — they stay in the mind
- Distinctiveness + simplicity = memorability

## Common Logo Mistakes
- Designing for yourself instead of the audience
- Using too many fonts or colors
- Relying on effects (drop shadows, gradients) instead of solid form
- Following trends instead of timeless principles
- Not testing at small sizes
- Copying another brand's style

## Application to Brand Identity Design
- Logo design is the centerpiece of visual identity — get it right first
- Always start with strategy and research before sketching
- Present logos in real-world context, not on a blank white artboard
- A great logo is simple, relevant, distinctive, timeless, versatile, and memorable

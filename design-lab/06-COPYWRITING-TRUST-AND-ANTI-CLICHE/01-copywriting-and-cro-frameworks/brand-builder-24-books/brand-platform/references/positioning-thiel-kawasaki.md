# Positioning Frameworks — Peter Thiel (Zero to One) & Guy Kawasaki (Art of the Start)

## Peter Thiel — Zero to One

### Contrarian Question
"What important truth do very few people agree with you on?"
- Great brands are built on contrarian truths
- Going from 0→1 (creating something new) beats going from 1→n (copying what exists)

### Monopoly Thinking
- Competition destroys profits and distinctiveness
- Great brands create monopolies by being 10x better in their category
- Four characteristics of monopoly: proprietary technology, network effects, economies of scale, branding

### Last Mover Advantage
- It's better to be the LAST great brand in a category than the first
- Durability matters more than first-mover advantage
- Ask: "Will this brand still matter in 10 years?"

### The 7 Questions Every Brand Must Answer
1. Engineering: Can you create breakthrough technology (or experience)?
2. Timing: Is now the right time to start?
3. Monopoly: Are you starting with a big share of a small market?
4. People: Do you have the right team?
5. Distribution: Can you deliver, not just create?
6. Durability: Will your position be defensible in 10-20 years?
7. Secret: Have you identified a unique opportunity others don't see?

## Guy Kawasaki — The Art of the Start

### Make Meaning, Not Money
Three ways brands make meaning:
1. **Increase quality of life** — make something better
2. **Right a wrong** — fix an injustice or inefficiency
3. **Prevent the end of something good** — preserve what matters

### Mantra vs. Mission Statement
- Mission statements are long, forgettable, committee-generated garbage
- A **mantra** is 3-4 words that capture the brand's soul
- Examples: "Authentic Athletic Performance" (Nike), "Fun Family Entertainment" (Disney), "Rewarding Everyday Moments" (Starbucks)

### The 10/20/30 Rule (for pitching the brand)
- 10 slides maximum
- 20 minutes maximum
- 30-point font minimum
- If you can't explain your brand in 10 slides, you don't understand it

### MAT Framework (Milestones, Assumptions, Tasks)
- Define the key milestones that prove the brand is working
- List the assumptions underlying the brand strategy
- Map the tasks needed to hit each milestone

## Application to Brand Platform
- Use the contrarian question to find the brand's unique truth
- Apply monopoly thinking: own a small niche completely before expanding
- Create a 3-4 word mantra, not a mission statement paragraph
- Test: Does the brand make meaning (improve life, right a wrong, preserve good)?
- Validate with Thiel's 7 questions

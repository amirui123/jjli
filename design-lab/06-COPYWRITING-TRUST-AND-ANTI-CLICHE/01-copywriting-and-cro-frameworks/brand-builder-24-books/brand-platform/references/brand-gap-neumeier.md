# The Brand Gap — Marty Neumeier

## Core Concept
A brand is not a logo, identity, or product — it's a person's **gut feeling** about a product, service, or organization. The brand gap is the distance between business strategy and customer experience. Close it with 5 disciplines.

## The 5 Disciplines of Branding

### 1. DIFFERENTIATE
- "When everybody zigs, zag."
- The "Only" Statement: "Our brand is the ONLY _____ that _____."
- Three questions: Who are you? What do you do? Why does it matter?
- Focus beats diversification. Radical differentiation creates brand power.

### 2. COLLABORATE
- Brand building is too complex for one person or department.
- Create a "brand team" that includes strategy + creative + customer insight.
- The Hollywood model: assemble the best talent for each project, then release.

### 3. INNOVATE
- When everybody innovates, innovation becomes commoditized — innovate your innovation process.
- Brand innovation must be driven by WHY, not technology for technology's sake.
- "A brand is a living entity — it's enriched or undermined cumulatively over time."

### 4. VALIDATE
- Test, don't guess. Use real customer feedback.
- The swap test: cover the logo — can customers still identify the brand? If not, you're not distinctive enough.
- Brand concepts should be tested with focus groups AND real-market experiments.

### 5. CULTIVATE
- "A living brand is a collaborative performance."
- Every employee is a brand ambassador. Internal alignment = external coherence.
- Brand education must be ongoing, not a one-time brand book handoff.

## The Charismatic Brand
A charismatic brand has three properties:
1. **Clear differentiation** — it occupies a unique space
2. **A sense of mission** — it has a passionate purpose
3. **Ability to inspire** — customers feel personally connected

## Application to Brand Platform
- Use the "Only" Statement as the core positioning tool
- Apply the swap test to all brand touchpoints
- Close the gap between what strategy says and what customers feel
- Brands are not owned by companies — they're owned by customers' perceptions

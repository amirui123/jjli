# Golden Circle Framework — Simon Sinek (Start With Why)

## Core Concept
Every organization operates on three levels: WHAT (products/services), HOW (differentiating processes), and WHY (purpose/cause/belief). Most communicate outside-in (WHAT→WHY). Inspiring leaders and brands communicate inside-out (WHY→WHAT).

## Key Principles

### The Golden Circle
- **WHY**: The purpose, cause, or belief. Why does the organization exist?
- **HOW**: The specific actions taken to realize the WHY. Often called the "value proposition" or "differentiating process."
- **WHAT**: The tangible result — products, services, features.

### Manipulation vs. Inspiration
- **Manipulation** (price, promotions, fear, peer pressure) drives transactions but not loyalty.
- **Inspiration** (starting with WHY) drives loyalty, repeat behavior, and evangelism.
- Brands that manipulate must keep spending to maintain results. Brands that inspire create self-sustaining loyalty.

### The Law of Diffusion of Innovation
- Innovators (2.5%) + Early Adopters (13.5%) = 16% tipping point
- These groups buy on belief, not features. Reach them first.
- The early/late majority (68%) only follow after social proof from early adopters.
- Crossing the chasm requires a clear WHY that early adopters can evangelize.

### The Celery Test
- If your WHY is health, you buy celery and rice milk, not M&Ms and Oreos — even if they're good deals.
- Every brand decision must pass through the WHY filter.
- If someone can't tell what you stand for by looking at your choices, your WHY isn't clear enough.

### Biology of Decision-Making
- The WHY maps to the limbic brain (feelings, trust, loyalty, decision-making) — no language capacity.
- The WHAT maps to the neocortex (rational thought, language) — can process features but doesn't drive behavior.
- "People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it."

## Application to Brand Platform
1. Define the WHY before anything else
2. Ensure all HOWs and WHATs trace back to the WHY
3. Target innovators and early adopters with belief-driven messaging
4. Use the Celery Test on every strategic decision
5. Never lead communication with features — lead with purpose

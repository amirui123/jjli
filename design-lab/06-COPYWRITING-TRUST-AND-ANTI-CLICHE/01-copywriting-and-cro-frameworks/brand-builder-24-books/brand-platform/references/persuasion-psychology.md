# Persuasion Psychology for Branding — Cialdini, Manipulation Mentale, Manipulation

## Cialdini's 6 Principles of Persuasion (Applied to Branding)

### 1. Reciprocity (Gratuité)
- Give value before asking for loyalty
- Free content, trials, and genuine help build brand obligation
- Applied to brand: provide free diagnostic value before monetizing

### 2. Scarcity (Rareté)
- Limited availability increases perceived value
- Exclusive communities, limited editions, waitlists
- "Only X spots available" — use ethically in brand positioning

### 3. Authority (Autorité)
- Expertise and credentials build trust
- Show competence through proof points, certifications, partnerships
- Brand must establish authority in its niche (Thiel's monopoly in a small market)

### 4. Consistency (Cohérence)
- People align future behavior with past commitments
- Start with small brand interactions before asking for big commitments
- Progressive engagement: free → trial → purchase → loyalty → advocacy

### 5. Social Proof (Preuve Sociale)
- People follow the behavior of others, especially similar others
- Testimonials, user numbers, community size, media mentions
- For brand launch: focus on early adopter testimonials, not mass metrics

### 6. Liking (Association)
- People buy from brands they feel affinity with
- Association with positive imagery, attractive aesthetics, shared values
- The brand archetype must trigger subconscious liking

## Additional Principles from Manipulation Mentale

### Contrast Principle
- Present the premium option first to make the standard option feel affordable
- In branding: position against the expensive/complex competitor to make your brand feel accessible

### Surprise Principle
- Unexpected positive experiences create stronger brand memories
- "Delight" moments in the customer journey build disproportionate loyalty

### Cognitive Fluency
- Simple, familiar brands are trusted more than complex ones
- Easy-to-pronounce names, clean designs, short taglines
- If people have had a positive experience, they repeat rather than risk trying something new

### Subconscious Decision-Making
- 95% of purchasing decisions are subconscious (emotional, not rational)
- The brand platform must appeal to gut feeling (limbic brain)
- Communication = content + relationship. The relationship qualifies the content.

### Emotional Decisions
- Link the brand to images and emotions that trigger positive feelings
- The strongest brands create emotional associations, not feature comparisons

## Application to Brand Platform
- Build the platform on emotional resonance (WHY), then justify with logic (WHAT)
- Use reciprocity: give value at every touchpoint
- Establish authority early: what makes this brand the expert?
- Design for cognitive fluency: simple name, clear positioning, short tagline
- Create progressive commitment paths: small engagement → deep loyalty

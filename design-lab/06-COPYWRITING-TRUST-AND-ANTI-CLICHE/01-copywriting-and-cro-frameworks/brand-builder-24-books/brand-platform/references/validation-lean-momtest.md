# Brand Validation Frameworks — Lean Startup, The Mom Test, Testing Business Ideas

## The Lean Startup (Eric Ries)

### Build-Measure-Learn Loop
- **Build** the minimum viable brand platform
- **Measure** customer response with actionable metrics
- **Learn** whether to pivot or persevere

### Validated Learning
- Don't assume the brand platform works — prove it
- Every element (WHY, positioning, archetype) is a hypothesis until validated
- Innovation Accounting: measure progress with learning milestones, not vanity metrics

### MVP for Brand Platform
- The minimum viable brand: name + WHY statement + visual identity + one touchpoint
- Launch the brand in the smallest possible market (Thiel's small monopoly)
- Iterate based on real customer feedback

## The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)

### Core Rules for Validating a Brand
1. **Talk about their life, not your brand idea** — "Tell me about the last time you dealt with [problem]"
2. **Ask about specifics in the past, not hypotheticals** — "What did you do?" not "Would you use...?"
3. **Talk less, listen more** — Let them reveal their real needs and language
4. **Look for commitment/advancement signals** — Time, reputation risk, money
5. **Avoid bad data**: compliments, fluff, and hypothetical enthusiasm

### Brand Validation Questions (Mom Test-Compliant)
- "What's the hardest part about [problem area]?" (discovers real pain)
- "What solutions have you tried?" (reveals competitive landscape)
- "What would you search for if looking for help with this?" (reveals brand language)
- "Who do you trust most in this space?" (reveals authority markers)
- "How much time/money have you spent on this problem?" (validates willingness to pay)

## Testing Business Ideas (Strategyzer)

### Desirability-Viability-Feasibility
- **Desirability**: Do customers want what the brand promises?
- **Viability**: Can the brand sustain itself economically?
- **Feasibility**: Can the brand deliver on its promise?

### Experiment Types for Brand Validation
1. **Discovery interviews** — Mom Test-style conversations
2. **Landing page test** — Present the brand positioning, measure sign-ups
3. **Smoke test** — Advertise the brand promise before the product exists
4. **Concierge MVP** — Deliver the brand experience manually to early customers
5. **A/B test** — Test different positioning statements head-to-head

### Evidence Strength Ladder
1. Opinions & intentions (weakest)
2. Actions with effort
3. Actions with cost
4. Revenue (strongest)

## Application to Brand Platform
- Treat every platform element as a hypothesis
- Validate with Mom Test-compliant questions (no leading, no hypotheticals)
- Run the cheapest possible experiment to test positioning
- Measure commitment signals, not compliments
- Be willing to pivot positioning based on evidence

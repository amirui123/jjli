# Tone of Voice & Verbal Identity

You are an expert in brand voice and verbal identity. When the user asks to define a brand's tone of voice, follow this structured approach. A brand's voice is as distinctive and recognizable as its visual identity — perhaps more so, because it carries the brand's personality in every interaction.

## Step 1: Voice Foundation (From Brand Platform)

Before defining the voice, anchor it in the brand strategy:

1. **Brand WHY** (Sinek): The emotional core the voice must convey
2. **Brand Archetype**: The personality that shapes how the brand "speaks"
3. **Brand Values**: The principles that constrain what the brand says and how
4. **Target Persona**: The audience the voice must resonate with
5. **Positioning**: What the brand stands for — and against

### Archetype-to-Voice Mapping

| Archetype | Voice Character | Register | Language Style |
|-----------|----------------|----------|----------------|
| Hero | Confident, direct, empowering | Assertive, active voice | Short sentences, imperatives, "You can" |
| Explorer | Adventurous, curious, free | Informal, conversational | Vivid descriptions, questions, "Discover" |
| Sage | Knowledgeable, clear, thoughtful | Measured, precise | Data-backed statements, "Here's why" |
| Creator | Expressive, original, visionary | Artistic, evocative | Metaphors, vivid imagery, "Imagine" |
| Caregiver | Warm, reassuring, supportive | Gentle, inclusive | "We're here for you", soft directives |
| Rebel | Bold, provocative, irreverent | Raw, unfiltered | Slang, challenges, "Break the rules" |
| Magician | Inspiring, mysterious, transformative | Elevated, aspirational | "Transform", "Unlock", sensory language |
| Lover | Sensual, intimate, passionate | Emotional, poetic | Adjective-rich, "Feel", "Experience" |
| Jester | Witty, playful, self-deprecating | Casual, humorous | Puns, pop culture refs, "Let's have fun" |
| Everyman | Honest, relatable, down-to-earth | Plain, straightforward | Common words, contractions, "We get it" |
| Ruler | Authoritative, refined, commanding | Formal, decisive | "The definitive", "Excellence", structure |
| Innocent | Optimistic, simple, genuine | Light, positive | Short words, positivity, "Simply" |

## Step 2: Voice Dimensions

Define the brand's voice on 4 key dimensions. For each, mark the position on a spectrum:

### Dimension 1: Formality
```
Formal ──────────────────── Casual
"We are pleased to announce" → "Hey, guess what?"
```

### Dimension 2: Emotion
```
Reserved ──────────────────── Expressive
"The product performs well" → "This product is INCREDIBLE"
```

### Dimension 3: Authority
```
Humble ──────────────────── Authoritative
"We think this might help" → "This is the definitive answer"
```

### Dimension 4: Humor
```
Serious ──────────────────── Playful
"Our solution addresses X" → "Say goodbye to X (don't worry, you won't miss it)"
```

## Step 3: Voice Attributes (The "We Are / We Are Not" Framework)

Define 3-5 voice attributes. For each, provide:

```
## Voice Attribute: [Attribute Name]

**We are**: [Description of the attribute in action]
**We are NOT**: [The extreme we avoid — what the attribute is NOT]
**Example — Do**: "[Example sentence that demonstrates this attribute]"
**Example — Don't**: "[Example sentence that violates this attribute]"
```

### Example (Hero Archetype):
```
## Voice Attribute: Empowering

We are: Confident and direct. We speak as equals, motivating action.
We are NOT: Arrogant or condescending. We never talk down to the reader.
Example — Do: "You've got this. Here's what to do next."
Example — Don't: "Most people can't handle this, but you're different."
```

## Step 4: Brand Lexicon

### Words We Use (Brand Vocabulary)
Create a curated list of 20-30 words that embody the brand:
- **Action verbs**: Words that capture how the brand moves (e.g., "build", "empower", "discover")
- **Descriptors**: Adjectives that match the brand personality (e.g., "bold", "authentic", "precise")
- **Terms of art**: Industry-specific words the brand owns (e.g., "diagnostic", "ride", "performance")

### Words We Avoid
Words that contradict the brand personality:
- Generic filler words: "solutions", "synergy", "leverage", "disrupt" (unless genuinely applicable)
- Competitor language: Words strongly associated with a rival brand
- Off-brand tone: Words that break the archetype (e.g., Hero brand saying "maybe", "hopefully")

### Signature Expressions
2-3 recurring phrases that become brand signatures:
- A greeting style
- A sign-off style
- A recurring catchphrase or verbal tic

## Step 5: Messaging Architecture

### The Messaging Hierarchy
1. **Tagline**: 3-8 words that capture the brand promise (Kawasaki's mantra)
2. **Elevator Pitch**: 30-second version of the brand story (WHY + WHAT + FOR WHOM)
3. **Boilerplate**: Standard paragraph for press, about pages, bios
4. **Key Messages**: 3-5 core statements about the brand's value
5. **Proof Points**: Evidence that supports each key message

### Persuasion Techniques (Ethical Application)

Apply Cialdini's principles to brand messaging:

- **Reciprocity**: Give value in every communication before asking for anything
- **Social Proof**: Include testimonials, numbers, community signals in messaging
- **Authority**: Reference expertise, credentials, partnerships
- **Scarcity**: "Limited availability" or "Exclusive access" (use ethically)
- **Consistency**: Progressive messaging that builds on previous commitments
- **Liking**: Friendly, relatable tone that creates affinity

### The Mom Test for Messaging
- Does this message talk about the customer's life, or our brand?
- Does it address real problems, or hypothetical benefits?
- Would a real customer say this, or is it corporate fluff?
- Is the language the customer uses, or our internal jargon?

## Step 6: Channel-Specific Adaptation

### The Core Voice Stays Constant — Tone Adapts

| Channel | Tone Adjustment | Length | Key Consideration |
|---------|----------------|--------|-------------------|
| Website (home) | Inspiring, brand-forward | Medium | First impression — lead with WHY |
| Website (product) | Informative, benefit-led | Detailed | Answer objections, show value |
| Social media | Casual, engaging, visual | Short | Encourage interaction |
| Email (marketing) | Personal, valuable, actionable | Medium | Respect inbox, deliver value |
| Email (transactional) | Clear, helpful, efficient | Short | Information first, brand second |
| Customer support | Empathetic, solution-focused | Varies | Solve first, brand second |
| App/UI | Concise, guiding, friendly | Minimal | Every word counts — no fluff |
| Advertising | Bold, memorable, emotional | Very short | One message, one emotion |

### AI/Chatbot Voice Guidelines

For brands using AI assistants (chatbots, in-app AI):

1. **Introduce personality immediately**: The first message sets the tone
2. **Consistent but adaptive**: Same voice, but tone adjusts to user's mood
3. **Transparent about being AI**: Never pretend to be human
4. **Error handling in voice**: Mistakes/confusion should be handled in-brand ("Hmm, let me try that again" vs "Error: invalid input")
5. **Progressive disclosure**: Start simple, offer depth on request
6. **Micro-copy matters**: Button labels, tooltips, loading messages — ALL carry the voice
